Research

I work on fair, reproducible benchmarking of vehicle controllers in simulation as well as real-world validation on EDGAR, TUM’s autonomous research vehicle. My recent work deals with developing a framework that compares controllers under identical scenarios, vehicle interfaces, and tuning conditions.

In my master’s thesis I developed a three-dimensional tube MPC for autonomous racing, and in course projects I worked on visual odometry and diffusion policies for robot manipulation. I am interested in doing research on learning-based controller tuning methods as well as efficient algorithm configuration. I am also very interested in Lie algebra, which I discovered in my courses on computer vision and visual navigation.

Industry project

Open Framework for Autoware Evaluation

Team lead

A benchmarking framework, built with TIER IV within the TUM Autoware Research Center, that lets third parties test Autoware-based driving stacks against standardised scenarios. I coordinate a team of five doctoral researchers, am the main contact for TIER IV and build the controller evaluation pipeline.

We presented the project at the 1st Autoware Workshop and Tutorial, IEEE ITSC 2026, NaplesAnnouncementWorkshop
